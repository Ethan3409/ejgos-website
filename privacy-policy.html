Privacy Policy

Effective October 6, 2026 Version 2.0 Includes our Messaging Terms and Conditions

EJG Technologies Inc. (“EJG”, “we”) designs, installs and supports EJG-OS, a smart home system that runs on a hub installed in your house. This policy explains what personal information is handled, where it goes and why, across everything we run: this website, your EJG-OS hub, the EJG Home iPhone and wall-tablet apps, the text messages we send, and the access your installer has to your system. Most of your home's information stays on the hub. Where something does leave, this policy names who receives it. This page is our combined Privacy Policy and Messaging Terms and Conditions.

The short versionWhat stays home, and what doesn't.

  • Stays on your hub: your devices and their history, rooms, scenes, automations, household user accounts, lock codes, Skip's household profiles and memories, camera events and any recordings the hub makes, and face data if you use face recognition. Lights, locks, thermostats, scenes and automations work with the internet unplugged.
  • Leaves your home: the Skip voice assistant (on by default on the wall tablet: speech recognition by Google or Apple; requests the hub can't handle itself go to Anthropic through a relay we run on Cloudflare; spoken replies can be produced by OpenAI), phone notifications (Expo, Apple, Google), remote access (EJG Secure Link or Cloudflare Tunnel), weather, Spotify if you link it, encrypted backup copies sent to your installer's computer, and your home's record in your installer's account (see “Installer access”). Each is described below.
  • Never: we do not sell your personal information, show you ads, or put analytics, advertising or tracking code in our apps or on this website.

This websiteNo analytics, no ads, no trackers.

  • Hosting. ejgos.com is hosted by Netlify and served through Cloudflare. Like any web host, they process your IP address and browser details to deliver pages and protect the site from attacks.
  • Forms. When you send a contact, quote, builder or installer form, the details you type are delivered to our inbox by FormSubmit (formsubmit.co). We use them only to reply to you and prepare a quote. The contact form is described in more detail below.
  • Phone and text messages. Our business phone number and text messages run through Grasshopper, our phone and text-messaging provider.
  • Nothing else. The website loads no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts, and we set no cookies of our own.

Your hubThe home's records live in the house.

Your EJG-OS hub is a small computer installed in your home and under your physical control. It stores: your devices, their states and history; rooms, scenes and automations; household user accounts and roles; lock user codes; system logs; camera settings, events and any recordings the hub makes; and the household profiles and memories Skip uses. A profile can include a name, age and preferences, and anything else your household chooses to tell Skip. This information is not copied to servers run by EJG, except as described under “Installer access” and “Backups” below.

The EJG Home appsOnly what's needed to talk to your hub.

  • Login credentials. Stored in your device's encrypted secure storage — the iOS Keychain on iPhone, and Android's encrypted secure storage (SecureStore, backed by the Android Keystore) on the tablet — and sent only to your own hub to sign in.
  • Hub address. The local or remote-access address of your hub, stored on your device.
  • Device identifier. A random ID your hub uses to recognise this phone or tablet. It is not used for advertising or shared for tracking.
  • Push notification token. Issued by Apple or Google so your hub can send you alerts (see “Notifications”).
  • Not collected. No advertising identifiers, analytics or behavioural telemetry, no location tracking, and no access to your contacts, calendar or photo library.

Permissions requestedOnly what each feature needs.

  • Network access — to reach your EJG-OS hub.
  • Local network (iPhone) — to talk to your hub directly on your home network.
  • Microphone and speech recognition — for Skip. On iPhone, only after you enable Skip; while Skip is on, the iPhone may keep listening in the background. On the wall tablet, Skip is on when we install it (see below). You can revoke these permissions at any time in your device's settings.
  • Camera — on iPhone, to scan setup codes (a Secure Link invitation or a Z-Wave device code); on the wall tablet, to take a face-recognition enrolment photo if you use that feature.
  • Notifications — only if you opt in when prompted.
  • Wake lock (tablet only) — to keep the wall panel display on.

Skip, the voice assistantOn by default on the wall tablet; it needs the internet today.

  • Speech recognition. Turning your speech into text is done by your device's built-in speech service: Google's on the Android wall tablet, and Apple's on iPhone. Your audio may be processed on Apple's or Google's servers under their privacy policies.
  • The wall tablet listens continuously. On the wall tablet, Skip is on when we install it. While it listens, the tablet sends sound from the room to Google's speech service continuously — not only after “Hey Skip” — and the text that comes back is written to your hub's log, which your installer can see during approved access. Mute the tablet or turn Skip off in Settings to stop this.
  • Everyday commands. Simple commands — lights, locks, the thermostat, scenes — are understood and carried out by your hub where it can.
  • Other requests. Requests your hub can't handle itself are sent to our AI provider, Anthropic (Claude), through a relay we operate on Cloudflare. A request can include what was said; the speaker's first name, age, pronouns and profile notes; memories Skip keeps, including other household members' memories and family messages; your city or region and time zone; the state of your home's devices, recent activity and camera event summaries; and your home's ID. Anthropic may run a web search to answer. Our relay keeps basic logs (your home's ID and the type of request), not the content; Anthropic processes requests under its commercial terms and privacy policy.
  • AI scene and automation builders. When an AI builder is used to draft a scene or automation, the request and your devices' names, rooms and types are sent to Anthropic the same way.
  • Fun answers. For recipes, trivia and fun facts Skip may ask TheMealDB, Open Trivia DB or uselessfacts; these requests carry the question topic, not your personal details.
  • Skip's voice. Skip's spoken replies are produced either by a voice that runs on your hub (Piper) or by OpenAI. When OpenAI is used, the text of Skip's reply is sent to OpenAI to produce the audio, under OpenAI's privacy policy.
  • Spotify. If you link Spotify, your hub stores the link and sends playback commands to Spotify under its privacy policy.

NotificationsDelivered by Expo, Apple and Google.

Your hub composes alerts and sends them through Expo's push service, which passes them to Apple Push Notification service (iPhone) or Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android). Those services receive your device's push token and the notification text. Depending on your settings and the kind of alert, the text can include details such as the name of a device, door or camera, or the name of an installer asking for access. We do not read or store notification content on our servers.

Remote accessUsing your home from away.

  • EJG Secure Link. On homes using Secure Link, your phone and hub connect over a tunnel that is end-to-end encrypted between them. A coordination server we run on DigitalOcean (mesh.ejgos.com) helps devices find each other. It holds device names, network keys and connection details, not the contents of your traffic.
  • Cloudflare Tunnel. On homes that use a Cloudflare Tunnel, traffic is encrypted between your phone and Cloudflare and between Cloudflare and your hub, but it is decrypted at Cloudflare's servers in between. This includes camera video you watch from away. Cloudflare Tunnel is the default for homes not on Secure Link, and those homes have a public address of the form name.ejgos.com.
  • At home. On your home network, the app talks to the hub directly.

Cameras and face recognitionRecorded in the house, viewed by you.

  • Recordings. Camera video is recorded on equipment inside your home: a network video recorder (NVR). We turn off the recorder's cloud features. Nobody at EJG monitors your cameras. The recorder overwrites the oldest footage when its drive is full. The hub also keeps motion recordings (30 days by default; flagged events 90 days) when it can reach your cameras.
  • Viewing from away. Video you watch from away reaches your phone through your remote-access connection (see “Remote access”).
  • Face recognition. Optional. If you turn it on, faces are analysed by software running on your hub, and the face data for people you enrol (a reference photo, their name, and when they were last recognised) is stored on the hub. Events with faces nobody has named are deleted after 30 days by default. Enrol only people who have agreed to it, and delete their face data when they ask.
  • Your responsibilities. You decide where cameras point. Let household members, guests and anyone who works in your home know that cameras record, avoid pointing cameras at neighbours' property or public areas, and follow the laws that apply to recording in your home.

Other servicesUsed only when the feature is in use.

  • Weather. Your hub sends your home's location to a weather service (Environment Canada, Open-Meteo or MET Norway) to get local weather, and may use Open-Meteo's geocoding service to turn a place name into coordinates.
  • Tuya and other makers' devices. Tuya devices are linked through Tuya's cloud once, at setup. After that, a fallback to Tuya's cloud (if your hub can't reach a device locally) is off unless it is switched on; when on, device status and commands pass through Tuya. Some third-party devices also contact their own makers' servers on their own.
  • Z-Wave and Zigbee software. Where enabled, the Z-Wave software on your hub sends anonymous usage statistics (a list of device makes, models and firmware versions) to the Z-Wave JS project (zwave-js.io). Where a Zigbee stick is installed, its software (deCONZ) uses Phoscon's discovery service (phoscon.de).
  • Home Assistant. Homes where we set up Home Assistant (for some plug-in devices) use its default online services: weather from MET Norway using your home's location, its alerts feed, Google Translate for its spoken voice, radio-browser for internet radio, and downloads of its software packages.
  • App updates. The EJG Home apps check Expo's update service for app updates, sending an install ID with the check.
  • Software, time and updates coming in. When your hub fetches system software or the time from the internet, those services see only your hub's internet address. EJG-OS software updates are digitally signed and delivered to your hub by your installer.
  • Health report. Your hub reports basic health (status codes and counts, not device names) to your installer over EJG Secure Link so they can support it. Ask us and we will switch it off.

Installer accessWhat your installer can reach, and what needs your approval.

Using the installer tools on your home — seeing and changing devices, rooms, scenes and settings — needs your approval in the app. You choose how long that access lasts, within the limits of your hub's security setting, and you can cancel it at any time. While access is on, the installer tools also show system health and logs. Your installer (EJG, or a partner installer we authorise) also holds a maintenance key and your hub's secret, used to deliver signed software updates, receive your hub's health report, and service the hub on site.

Your home's record in the installer's account. Your installer's account is kept in sync across their computers through an account service we run (acct.ejgos.com). Each home's record there includes your address and location, your hub's network addresses, your hub's secret and your Z-Wave network keys. The record is encrypted, but with a key derived from the installer's password, which the account server receives today when the installer signs in.

We also keep the records needed to run your account: your name and contact details, address, the equipment installed, quotes, invoices and support history. Quotes and invoices are emailed through Resend, which receives your name and email address.

BackupsEncrypted, and where the copies live.

Your hub makes an encrypted backup every night and keeps the most recent ones on its own storage, so it can be restored if a drive fails. A backup contains the hub's database (including Skip profiles and any face data), device pairing data and settings. Backups are encrypted so they can only be opened with your hub's key or your own recovery key. If your installer sets it up, an off-hub copy is also sent to your installer's computer over EJG Secure Link or your home network; that copy is encrypted to your recovery key only, so the installer stores it but cannot open it.

ChildrenHousehold profiles are set up by adults.

EJG Home accounts are created and managed by adults in the household, and our apps and website are not directed at children under 13. A parent or guardian may create a Skip profile for a child (for example, a name and age so Skip answers appropriately); it is stored on the hub. When a child talks to Skip, their request is handled the same way as anyone else's (see “Skip, the voice assistant”). A parent or guardian can review, change or delete a child's profile at any time.

Retention and deletionKept only as long as needed.

  • On your hub: until you delete it, or until the hub is reset. If you stop using EJG-OS or return hardware, we will securely erase the hub on request.
  • Enquiries: kept only as long as we need them to answer and follow up on your enquiry.
  • Customer records: quotes, invoices and billing records are kept as long as Canadian tax law requires (generally six years), then deleted.

Your rightsAccess, correction and complaints.

You can ask to see the personal information we hold about you and how it has been used and disclosed, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete what we don't need to keep. Contact our privacy officer; we will respond within the time Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act allows (generally 45 days). If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta or the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

Security and breachesHow we protect it, and what we do if something goes wrong.

Your hub uses role-based sign-in, a firewall, encrypted backups and digitally signed software updates, and remote access is encrypted in transit. If a loss of, unauthorised access to, or disclosure of personal information under our control creates a real risk of significant harm, we will notify the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta without unreasonable delay, notify the people affected, and keep a record of it.
